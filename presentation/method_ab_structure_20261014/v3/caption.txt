English caption

Overview of proposal refinement during federated long-tailed adaptation. The compact upper strip locates the frozen CLIP backbone, visual LoRA, and within-round order; each lower panel distinguishes ordinary local-update aggregation from feedback evaluated at a shared proposal. In B, clients holding tail-class data evaluate the same shared model and return globally weighted classification gradients for a bounded supplementary B update, with A fixed; tail classes receive equal total loss mass while non-tail coefficients remain unchanged. In A, reliable margins from committed models define capped retention targets, and client-side witness feedback combines retention and classification to refine the ordinary A proposal with B fixed; margin statistics return to the coordinator for history registration. Flames and snowflakes indicate trainable and frozen parameters, and all bars, history cells, and example images are schematic.

Protocol interpretation

The boxes show logical federated protocol roles, not a measured physical deployment. The current implementation is a federated simulation: a shared runtime can access client-partitioned datasets and uses centrally managed frozen-prefix caches. The drawing therefore does not establish that raw samples or features never enter shared process memory, or imply a privacy guarantee. Communication byte counters are protocol estimates rather than measured network traffic.

Raw training examples are associated with their originating clients in the logical diagram. Client evidence includes A gradients and per-witness two-view margin statistics; the shared runtime maintains SampleMarginHistory and updates the shared parameters. A history is not shown as private state held exclusively by individual clients. Only observations of actually committed models register history for later rounds; observations at intermediate proposals do not register it.

The ordinary A proposal is evaluated relative to registered history. A visible deficit is not proof that the current ordinary A update caused the deficit. The retained target is a training objective, not a guaranteed property of the final model or its test accuracy. The updated matrices deliberately show no fabricated successful repair, accuracy rise, or guaranteed forgetting reduction.

In B, joint aggregation combines completed local updates. The additional feedback is evaluated at a common shared model after aggregation, with that shared model updated between feedback steps. This is an operational distinction, not proof of independent empirical benefit over matched-budget ordinary feedback.

Schedules and details left out of the main diagram

Ordinary B: rounds 1–100; three local epochs each round.
Ordinary A: rounds 1–90; one local epoch each round, with updated B fixed.
B feedback: rounds 30, 40, ..., 100, after ordinary joint B aggregation and before ordinary A learning.
A extra refinement: requires valid history, a target deficit, and a nonzero radius; exactly three projected steps, with direct commitment of the third step.
Rounds 91–100 retain A and its history; B continues.
B executes two normalized projected gradient steps in effective coordinates with A fixed; it directly commits the second step.
Neither method uses an acceptance test, best-intermediate-step selection, or a fallback.

A_t and B_t denote factor states before the corresponding ordinary phase, not two separate inference models. A_ord/B_ord are ordinary joint-aggregated proposals; A_new/B_new are the committed outputs of their phases. The complete inference model is one shared adapted CLIP.
P_i = min(H_i, 0.01), where H_i is the registered positive historical margin; m_i schematically represents the current unscaled cosine margin on one witness view.
History uses two views and nonoverlapping five-round windows of committed models; positive initial two-view margins can also register history. Newly registered targets take effect in the next round.
A retention is evaluated over witnesses with valid history; A classification includes all fixed witnesses, including those without history. The full objective also penalizes displacement from A_ord.
A uses at most eight fixed training witnesses for each nonempty client-class, two deterministic views, and class-balanced global coefficients.
A's proposal radius is the unweighted RMS of ordinary local A update norms. B's radius follows the ordinary effective B update norm with rho=1.
LA denotes logit adjustment. Feedback gradients already contain the global sample coefficients and are summed without applying joint weights again.
The actual visual LoRA is rank 4 on Q/V in the final three vision-transformer blocks. Pretrained encoder weights and the text side are frozen.
Three client icons and three equal-weight bars are illustrative, not the actual numbers of clients or tail classes.
